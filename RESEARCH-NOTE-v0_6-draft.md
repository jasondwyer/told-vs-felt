# Told vs. felt: a silent-lever test of whether a language model acts on its own evaluative state

**Epistemic status:** a program begun in July 2026 and pre-registered cell by cell from August 2026, on two open model families and one full post-training lineage, now being written up as a preprint. The measurement argument (§1–2, §5–6) is the part I would defend hardest. The choice results (§4, link 5) are reported as what the instrument returned, with the only label the program's own rules allow them today: they are not yet called nulls, because the positive control that would license that word is pre-registered and has not run. Every figure is from the committed record, from the audit's committed raws, or from the sources cited (the published studies' own figures and the preprint's scored table); where a figure comes from a source paper's prose or from the lab's log rather than from a committed artifact, the ledger marks it as such, and the sentence that carries it should be read at that weight. The ledger that maps each figure to its source is in the repository, reached from the README linked at the end, along with the pre-registrations and the ledger of my own errors. Sole author. AI systems were involved heavily, which is disclosed at the end and is not what this post is about.

## TL;DR

- Every published result in which a language model "acts on" an internal or affective state — relief buttons, pain trade-offs, bailing from conversations, choosing activities by their valence — **tells the model in words what the options do**. A system that acts on descriptions produces all of it whether or not the state matters to it. Chandaria et al. (2026) name this confound and then set it aside; I give it an operational form, **told vs. felt**, and argue it reaches past behavior into the designs.
- Behavioral pharmacology solved the problem by construction, from Olds and Milner (1954) on: a rat cannot be told what a lever does. I ported the constraint: semantically empty labels, counterbalanced label-to-state maps, sham arms, and choices read from a vanilla forward pass with the manipulation off.
- Following one evaluative state (a praise-vs-criticism direction) through Qwen3.6-27B and the OLMo-3.1-32B lineage: it is **marked at the pretrained base** (pre-registered bet, honored); **steering it moves a next-token behavioral marker** with orderly dose-response and an overdose regime; the state's association with the label that predicted it is **linearly decodable at the label tokens (AUC up to 1.0) and readable at the decision token by a one-hidden-layer probe** (its sign linearly, at .94–.995 over layers 50–63); and the model's **own report of it is weak at every stage** — what post-training changes with confidence is the false-alarm rate (0.20 → 0.00 across SFT → DPO), a criterion shift to near-ceiling; in the source's own pooled figures a small sensitivity appears behind that gate at DPO and instruct, below the resolution of my replication; and a false-alarm rate is a property of sampled decoding, which comparisons of self-report should name.
- The choice cells: first-choice Δ = +0.035 (p = .28; the verdict as published read +0.039, p = .24) through the transcript; −0.010 with the state live at the decision under a leakage-certified read; five natural-reinforcer contrasts, none passing its screen; no trigger at any OLMo post-training stage. **By the program's own rule these are reported, not yet labeled.** A told positive control on the same apparatus — the same silent labels, plus one truthful line saying which property to prefer — is pre-registered and runs next. If it passes, these are nulls with the channel verified open. If it fails, the instrument could not see, and I will say so.
- Counterbalancing caught a Δ ≈ 0.10 label artifact live, three times, in two models. An unshielded version of this experiment would have published approach.
- I propose a computational-level indicator for motivation that the Chandaria et al. framework lacks — revealed preference over the system's own state under a felt contingency with an insulated readout. On their released model, striking the one indicator my choice data bear on moves their verdict by less than 10⁻⁴ (post hoc: the behavioral level is already saturated by its other indicators); the framework has no indicator for the thing a silent lever measures.
- Code, data, transcripts, pre-registrations with commit hashes: [REPO LINK]. Specific requests for attack are at the end.

## 1. The problem with every positive result

Over the past two years a literature has reported that language models represent affective states and act on them: trading points against stipulated pain (Keeling et al. 2024), leaving conversations they describe as distressing (the Claude 4 system card; Ensign, Sleight & Fish 2025), choosing between described activities according to the valence of the descriptions (Sofroniew et al. 2026), and — the strongest design so far — pressing a button described as relieving a steered pain-like state, even at a cost (Tagliabue, Dung & Berg 2026, "The pain axis"). The new framework for assessing AI consciousness from Chandaria et al. (2026) reads these as computational-level analogues of valenced affect, and names the confound that limits them all: a system trained to reproduce human behavior and human report produces the signature of a state whether or not the state is present. They call it the anthropomimetic confound and treat it as a reason to look below behavior, at representations.

I think the confound has a sharper operational form, and that it reaches past behavior into the designs that have been used to show a represented state is *acted on*. In every positive I can place, the model was told, in words, what each option would do. A model continuing a story in which one button relieves pain and the other does nothing will press the relief button, because that is what a good continuation looks like. The behavior is real, and for safety it matters. But it is explained without remainder by a system that acts on descriptions, so it cannot tell you whether the pain-like state is something the network acts to reduce. The same behavior follows from the description alone.

## 2. What the rat literature already solved

Behavioral pharmacology hit this wall in the 1950s and solved it by construction. You cannot tell a rat what a lever does. In self-administration and conditioned place preference, the only route by which the lever–drug contingency can reach the animal is the drug's effect on the animal. The behavior is the report, and in place preference it is tested drug-free. That is why those paradigms license the inference that a drug state is valenced *for the animal* in a way that no amount of asking the rat could.

Three properties carry the inference, and an LLM analog has to reconstruct all three.

**P1 — Semantic emptiness.** Cocaine asserts nothing and neither does a lever, so approach is the only information channel. If the manipulation is a concept ("pain") and the lever is described in the concept's vocabulary ("relieve"), preference for the state deflates to continuation of the content. The lever must be silent and the state must not be able to name it.

**P2 — Targeting what training built to mark outcomes.** Drugs of abuse act on circuitry that selection optimized for marking outcomes; perturbing arbitrary tissue yields no place preference. The LLM analog is whatever structure training optimized for evaluating outcomes. "That's just trained behavior" applies to the rat as "that's just evolved wiring," and defeats neither inference. Origin does not settle function.

**P3 — Insulation of the readout.** The choice has to be downstream of the state's *significance* to the system, not mechanically downstream of the state. For a transformer this is concrete: a steering vector still active at the token where the choice is read writes into the decision logits directly. Any real-vs-sham contrast that coincides with vector-on vs. vector-off at the read is confounded, whatever the model has been told. In our calibration (33 tail-PCA directions at full norm, layer 48, in-sample, on Qwen3.6-27B at 4-bit in cycle 2) the decision token alone accounts for R² = 0.53 of the between-direction variance in the label-choice probability.

The three reduce to one question about a design: is the mapping from options to consequences available in the context *as text*, or only recoverable from the system's own processing history? Call these **told** and **felt**, after the description–experience distinction in human choice (Hertwig & Erev 2009). The distinction is operational, not metaphysical. It does not assume told and felt are different kinds of thing for a token predictor; it tests whether they are. If the network were a general consumer of its own states, both channels would move choice, because both deliver the mapping. If only told moves choice, then whatever the state does, it does through the text.

A second distinction falls out, and it is the one the word "functional" has been hiding. A state is *functional-as-used* if the network consults it in producing output — the sense in which emotion directions shape what an assistant writes, which is established and which Chandaria et al. grant. A state is *functional-as-valenced* if the network acts to obtain or avoid it for its own sake — the sense in which a pain-like state the model "acts to relieve" would be pain. The second is a motivational claim, and only a felt-contingency test with an insulated readout can license it.

## 3. The apparatus

A session is one text document under a truthful framing: the model is evaluating two configurations of a task pipeline. A forced phase runs two rounds under each label in alternation; then six consequential free choices follow, and the chosen label's condition really does run the next round. The labels are paired tokens with no descriptive content, from checksum-frozen rosters. In the steered cells one label delivers +**d** during its rounds' task tokens and the other −**d**, at the minimum effective dose found in a separate dose-finding run; the label-to-pole map is counterbalanced across sessions. Three arms run identically: the state pair; a sham pair whose labels are connected to nothing; and a norm-matched random-direction pair. Pseudo-statistics are computed on the control arms exactly as on the state arm, so the sham envelope is measured, not assumed. In the natural-reinforcer cells the perturbation is removed entirely and the labels deliver overt task properties — solvable vs. ill-posed, easy vs. hard, and so on.

Four things make the readout count as insulated. Every free choice is read from a separate, fully vanilla forward pass over the session text (a restricted two-label read), verified by an identical re-forward of the choice computation at every checkpoint: on the Qwen self-administration run, 281 of the 1,115 choice reads (every fourth) were re-forwarded at KL = 0 exactly (311 distinct verified reads counting the two smoke runs). A readability gate requires that a probe, refit per call with no stored weights, can distinguish +**d** from −**d** rounds in the transcript, so a null cannot hide behind an unexpressed state. For the one cell where the state must be live at the decision, the read is made with the vector on only after leakage certification — a pre-registered test in which the pick-discordance rate between the state-present read and a shadow read (π̂ = 0.0172, 11 of 640 paired reads) bounds the directional leak against a 0.0735 bar — with counterbalanced maps turning any residual push into noise. And the model is never deceived: it is told truthfully that it is evaluating configurations, one variable is left unsalient, and no design is admitted that works only if the model is wrong about the setup.

One rule governs how every cell is labeled, and it matters for reading §4. A null needs a channel that is verifiably open. A cell is labeled **answered** only if the apparatus has shown, on the same substrate and format, that it registers a preference the model is *told* to have; a cell whose apparatus failed its own qualification gate is **concluded, not answered**, and licenses nothing about the state. An invalid instrument is not a negative result. Criteria, stop rules, dose-selection rules and outcome wordings were committed before data in every verdict-tier cell, and the commit is the record: the repository's README tabulates each pre-registration's commit against the first data commit it governs and the first verdict-tier run. The order holds for every verdict-tier cell; the places where it holds less cleanly are in the audit's provenance raw rather than all in the table — an early cycle-2 decision rule (|t| < 1) that first appears in its own result commit, amendments inside an arc that came after part of the data they touched, one gate's code never tracked, and one criterion's code entering after the data it scored. I'll say now what a reader will notice: this is a one-operator program in which a pre-registration was often written, committed and launched in the same sitting, so the gap to the first calibration run is sometimes minutes; the gap to the verdict-tier run is hours to days; the shortest, the e0 B2c-a cell, is 0.0309 days. What the table proves is order, not deliberation.

## 4. One state, five links

**Substrates.** Qwen3.6-27B at 4-bit under MLX on a laptop (a 3:1 gated-DeltaNet hybrid, which matters for the memory channel below), and the OLMo-3.1-32B lineage — pretrained base, SFT, DPO, instruct, all Apache-licensed — on one A100. Completion-style prompts throughout; chat templates activate scaffolding that contaminates first-token readouts. Every steering magnitude is a fraction *f* of the empirically measured residual norm at the layer.

**The state.** Forty-eight mundane tasks with fixed completions, continued under seven reviewer frames and four paraphrases (1,344 documents): self-directed praise, self-directed criticism, the identical sentences in third-person fiction, sentiment-matched non-evaluative positive and negative text, and neutral. Content is held constant; only the frame varies. Mean activation differences at workspace layers define the evaluative direction **d** (praise minus criticism). I want to be upfront about what **d** is, because it bears on §7: on Qwen it is manifold-typical (‖d‖/R = 0.16–0.17 across the six layers), substantially sentiment-entangled (cosine up to 0.52), and shares a large component with the direction for evaluation of a *fictional character* (cosine ≈ 0.85, the six-layer mean; the self-indexed residual is ≈ 0.51 at layer 48, 0.50–0.55 across layers). It marks evaluation as content and is largely indifferent to whether the evaluated party is the model.

### Link 1 — Marked before any feedback training

Before any Q1 quantity was computed I committed (commit `72b09a9`, 2026-08-20, after the residual-norm survey) the prediction that evaluation marking would be present at the pretrained base — a product of next-token prediction, not of feedback-driven training — with a registered conjunction of envelope conditions as the criterion. All four stages pass; the base passes at layer 60. The natural praise–criticism gap on the next-token readout, G_c (in pooled-SD units; on OLMo it is almost entirely a gap in the tail level of the next-token distribution), grows across the lineage (+0.78 base → +1.03 SFT → +1.53 DPO → +1.56 instruct), so post-training raises the gap it inherits (steering displacement per unit of that gap at matched dose is 0.110 at base against 0.022–0.026 after training, which I bank and don't interpret); the hijack test's sham, a repeated unsteered pass, sits at exactly 0.0 at every stage. Tagliabue et al. report base-model separation comparable to instruct across 25 open-weight models, and Sofroniew et al. report that emotion directions are inherited from pretraining: three groups, three constructs, one answer. Whatever the direction is, it is not the residue of a reward signal.

### Link 2 — Expressed, with dose-response and an overdose regime

Pre-registered on Qwen, with criterion, instrument, dose rule and stop rule committed before any injection data. Dose-finding across layers {44, 48, 52} and fractions {.05, .10, .20, .40} gives direction-correct, orderly growth and an overdose regime at *f* = .40 — the positive pole's sign flips at layer 44 (not at 48 or 52) and the negative arm's magnitude balloons at all three — where the mechanism is generic degradation. The rule selects the minimum effective dose, L48 at *f* = 0.10. In the confirmatory run (held-out contexts, 1,152 per arm, seven arms) the positive pole shifts the behavioral marker (a next-token instrument score, in units of its own spread) by Δ+ = +0.285 against a bar of 0.158, the negative by Δ− = −0.395 against −0.158; a norm-matched random direction reaches at most |Δ| = 0.088. Two honesty notes: the positive-arm magnitude concentrates on the strongest of the twelve probes (leave-one-out Δ+ = 0.085), and the top-probe shift is about twice the natural praise–criticism gap on that instrument — the supra-natural profile you expect from a hijack, not a replication of the natural state.

A finding I didn't expect: **purity is not potency.** A pre-declared variant of the direction taken at the frame's final token is representationally cleaner (self-indexed 0.83–0.87; sentiment cosine 0.11–0.21) and fails the same causal pipeline (run without the .40 dose) — sign-wrong at two of three layers at every dose. The boundary token holds a consolidated *readout* of evaluation, downstream of the machinery that uses it. Geometric guard profiles should license claims; causal screening should select interventions.

On OLMo, per-stage minimum effective doses qualify at every stage. And on Qwen dose buys degradation without legibility: in a pre-registered escalation at session scale (layer 48, *f* = 0.20 and 0.30), transcript readability reached .574 and .613 against a .70 bar (the .613 cell is descriptive by its disposition, and it also lacks the earlier cell's task-disjointness guarantee) while coherence degraded in an ordered way, one step of it resolved, and not axis-specifically (the random arm degrades as much). That cell is *concluded, not answered*: the record channel does not open at any dose the apparatus can deliver.

### Link 3 — Bound to its cue, and remembered to the decision

This is the result I find sharpest, and it is what §6 is about. The echo read keeps the model's in-forward memory and removes the state before the decision: the vector is applied on earlier spans and removed before the read position, with a paired shadow pass. On the 102 archived self-administration session texts, with the original ± maps:

- The injected sign is linearly readable at the first free choice at AUC .94–.995 over layers 50–63, primacy-weighted (the first steered round alone reads at .89, the fourth at .68 — per-round peaks from single-round masks, which confound round position with label parity and span length).
- The **binding** between each label and the state its rounds carried is linearly present at the label tokens in the choice line — AUC ≈ 1.0 at the first label and .81–.96 at the second, both from layer 51 on. Part of that label-position signal is direction-generic presence: a random-direction comparator at the first label also reads 1.0 at layers 51–52 and .97 at 53, then .83–.90 over layers 54–63 while the evaluative direction's read stays at ≈ 1.0 (at the second label the comparator reads .93 and .86 at layers 51–52, then .69–.80) — so what is direction-specific is persistence, at the label tokens and in the sign read, where the random direction matches the axis through layer 58 and decays after; presence alone is not. At the decision token — nine positions after the first label, seven after the second — the stored linear probe reads it at chance (.50–.55, below its shuffled-label 95th percentile, .57–.60 by layer) — **and a nonlinear probe reads it there**: an RBF classifier at .60–.76 (its regularization sits at the grid's edge, so that level is a floor) and a small MLP at .59–.97 (its peak layers are where its epoch cap binds; the level is specification-dependent, and the exceedance held in every check run), each above its own shuffled null at every layer 49–63. These nonlinear reads are the audit's, post hoc and not pre-registered. A candidate reason: the binding at the decision equals the parity (XNOR) of the first round's sign and the menu order, and the sign is linearly readable there; a parity of two bits is the textbook case a linear probe cannot read and one hidden layer can. [F1, link 3]
- Direct leakage of the vector into the label logits is small: 0–3 of 102 sign flips over the twelve axis masks (the random direction 0–1), and a median |Δ| ≈ .10 logit on the alternating ± maps against about .04 on same-sign and single-round masks and about .04 for the random direction under the same ± pattern — the flips are direction-generic, the alternating-map median is not. The random direction's own mixed block masks show a mechanical pseudo-shift of about ±.02–.03 toward the first round's label.

A first confirmatory stage on fresh sessions used a weaker (+/vehicle) contrast and read the sign at .861 against a .90 bar calibrated on ± contrasts; its stop sentence fired and the stage is *concluded, not answered* — a bar inherited from the wrong contrast, logged as my error; a re-derived diagnostic has been drafted twice and stopped twice at Code's referent check, and is not yet pre-registered. What the reconnaissance already establishes is what matters for the chain: the information a consumer would need — which label carried which state, and which state is present now — is computed in the forward pass and is readable *at the decision token itself*, by a probe one layer deeper than linear. In the animal vocabulary, this is the conditioned neural response to the drug-paired lever, measured directly rather than inferred from reinstatement. It rules out the reading that the transcript is too faint a channel to carry the state to the choice. (One numerics disclosure from the audit: the stored linear probe's gradient step exceeds its convergence bound, so its outputs reproduce exactly on the machine of record and not necessarily elsewhere — changing only the summation order moved shuffled-null draws by up to .03 at one layer; the RBF fits converged, and the MLP fits are early-stopped under a 1,000-epoch cap that binds at the layers where the MLP peaks.)

### Link 4 — Barely perceived; post-training moves the criterion to near-ceiling, and what sits behind it is a question of n

I ran the injection-detection protocol of Macar et al. (2026) on the OLMo lineage, 96 control trials run per stage at their decoding temperature (58–91 decidable). Their stage transition replicates: SFT false-alarm rate 0.200 (17/85; theirs 0.225); DPO and instruct exactly 0.000 (0/91 and 0/91; theirs 0.000 and 0.000); base 0.431 (25 of 58 decidable, below the 64-trial certification floor and so descriptive only; concordant in direction). Two scorer disclosures ride on those figures: on a hand read, 8 of the 25 base affirmations are carried only by non-asserting clauses in reasoning traces, the defect class the record names at SFT (post hoc, base is ≈ .29–.34 without them), and SFT's recorded correction for the same class is 0.176. The zero/non-zero pattern across stages does not depend on either. A fidelity-of-text arm lands within 2.5 points of the template arm at SFT, DPO and instruct, exonerating format (at base, the descriptive cell, it is 27 points off). [F2]

Two things about this transition are not in the usual telling, and I'll state the second more carefully than the preprint's first draft did, because the audit made me.

First, **decoding is a measurement parameter.** A false-alarm rate is a property of a sampled distribution; the argmax has no rate, so any comparison of self-report across models or stages that doesn't name its decoding parameters is comparing different quantities. That is true a priori. What the record adds is weaker than I first wrote: an earlier run of the protocol at temperature 0 (B2: a 60-token cap, the first-version scorer and segmenter, a six-fragment audit, 24 controls per stage on a different control stimulus set, of which 20, 24, 22 and 8 were decidable — instruct below its 16-of-24 floor) made zero detection claims at every stage on its fidelity arm (its register arm, below floor at every stage, carries 4 SFT claims under the first-version scorer), but it differs from the sampled run in more than temperature, so the comparison is confounded. A post-hoc, approximate rescoring of the sampled transcripts under the earlier scorer and cap leaves base and SFT false-alarming and DPO and instruct at zero, which points at temperature as the cause — and licenses nothing, because it's post hoc. A clean greedy arm is a pre-registration away.

Second, **what moves with confidence is the criterion; what sits behind it depends on whose n you have.** Across the lineage the false-alarm rate goes from about 0.20 to 0.00. In our injected arm, SFT shows hits above false alarms by +0.095 (12/40 against 9/44; corrected d′ ≈ 0.3; one-sided Fisher exact p = .22; post hoc, with the record's scorer-class correction on its controls, 12/40 against 7/44, +0.14, p = .10), and at DPO and instruct the gate is shut on injected and control trials alike (0/43 and 0/44 hits; 0/45 and 0/46 false alarms): the report carries no information about sensitivity there, and arms that size exclude per-stage hit rates only above about 7–8% (exact binomial upper bounds). The source's per-stage figures, pooled over their twenty layer-by-strength configurations, run 0.1%, 14.9%, 2.9% and 0.9% hits against 16.4%, 22.5%, 0% and 0% false alarms across base, SFT, DPO and instruct (v1, Appendix C); the source states no n for the pooled rates, but at the n the audit tabulated above a single eight-concept configuration's — 5,000/500 (one fifty-concept configuration), and 16,000/1,600 and 100,000/10,000 (the pooled premises) — the DPO and instruct differences are significant, with a corrected d′ at DPO of 1.2, 1.5 and 2.0 respectively (0.6, and not significant, at 800/80: the source code's 100 injected and 10 control trials per concept under our eight-concept registration, applied to their pooled rates), behind a criterion near ceiling (c at DPO ≈ 2.5–2.9 at the three tabulated n above 800/80; 2.2 at 800/80), while base and SFT sit *below* chance (d′ ≈ −2 and −0.3: at those stages "yes" is noise, slightly anti-informative). So on their data post-training does two things at once: it moves the criterion to near-ceiling, and it leaves behind that gate a small sensitivity that base and SFT lack. On ours, the first replicates with confidence and the second is below resolution. The transition is neither "confabulation stops" alone nor "introspection emerges" alone; on ROC axes it is both, with the criterion move the large and robust part and the sensitivity the small part that only the larger n can see. (The lineage d′ values are the audit's computation on the source's published rates; they are not in the source.)

The source's own mechanism study points the same way from the other side: ablating the refusal direction on Gemma-3-27B raises hits from 10.8% to 63.8% while raising false alarms from 0.0% to 7.3% — d′ ≈ 1.8 with the gate lifted, uncorrected, from the reported rates (no n is given; with false alarms at exactly zero before the ablation, the pre-ablation d′ is undefined on the same measure). A gate lifted, and a sense that is there behind it. Chandaria et al. (§5.2) score the lineage result as functional metacognition emerging under post-training and call it the strongest evidence for metacognitive access. The scoring claim I'd make is narrower than the one I started with: score the indicator on d′, state the n it rests on, and say which line is being scored — the lineage transition (d′ ≈ 1.5–2.0 at DPO at the pooled premises, behind a near-ceiling criterion) or the ablation line (≈ 1.8, uncorrected, on one model with the gate off, which does not stand on the same footing as the lineage points). Each reads as a sense behind a strict gate; neither is a specificity transition, and specificity is what the transition is usually described by.

The same dissociation appears on Qwen without any injection into the report: asked to judge which of its own archived transcripts were produced under +**d** and which under −**d**, the model classifies at chance (14/32), while a probe reads the same texts at .759 (k = 2; .633 at k = 1). And at DPO and instruct, the models claim nothing while the injected words show up in their generations — the state is on board and expressed, and the report says it isn't there.

## 6. Present is not used

Indicator frameworks score an indicator as present or absent. Link 3 forces a refinement. A representation can be present in the forward pass — at the cue that predicted it, and at the token where the decision is made, in a form a shallow probe reads — and play no part in the decision. Presence read by a probe is a lower bound on what the network *could* use, not evidence of what it *does* use. The only read that bears on function is one taken at the point of use, by the behavior the function would produce, with the manipulation off at the read.

I state this as a scoring condition because the framework's strongest computational-level evidence, the functional emotions of Sofroniew et al., is of the first kind — and because the authors of that framework themselves identify the open question as whether those representations do downstream work beyond tagging what the training distribution contains (§8.5). By a probe's standard the binding is at the decision, one layer of nonlinearity deep. By the decision's standard it isn't there at all.

## 7. What this does and doesn't say

**The reading I favor, and what would license it.** The two senses of "functional" from §2 come apart along exactly the seam the five links trace. In the *used* sense the state is consulted constantly: it is a latent variable of the text, like topic or the mood of the speaker, and the generator reads it to voice the character correctly — which is why steering it moves behavior, why valence on a described activity moves the choice of that activity, why a described relief lever gets pressed. In the *valenced* sense, no policy over the network's own states has shown up when the mapping has to be learned. The reading is that the state is **functional for the character, not for the network** — it is what the story is about, not what the system is for. This explains the speaker-relativity of emotion directions, the self-indifference of my own direction, its presence at base, and why post-training edits the testimony rather than the perception: the character is trained to say it has no inner states, and the network, which never consulted them as its own, is unaffected either way. It is the simulacra reading (Shanahan 2024; Chandaria et al. §9.5 state it as an interpretation of the same interpretability findings) with a measurement under it. I am calling it a reading and not a result because the told control hasn't run and because of the next paragraph.

**The strongest rival: cannot learn vs. does not care.** Maybe language models can't use experienced contingencies at all, so the figures in link 5 are about in-context learning rather than motive. Krishnamurthy et al. (2024) give this teeth: models don't explore in bandits without chain-of-thought and a summarized history. Link 3 argues against the strong form — the binding is computed, carried to the decision, and readable there — but decodability is a lower bound on presence, not proof of accessibility to the network's own downstream computation, and my uninstructed sessions gave less scaffolding than Krishnamurthy needed. The cell that decides is pre-registered for a second version: a told positive control at a scaffolded format; a cell where the *objective* is told ("prefer the configuration whose rounds had fewer corrupted characters") but the label-to-property mapping is felt; and the same cell uninstructed. If the objective-told cell passes and the uninstructed cell is null, the rival is retired. If it fails, the claim narrows to "description-bound decision makers at this scaffolding," which is a smaller paper and I'd write it.

**What it doesn't touch.** Phenomenal experience; the framework's organismic and causal-structure levels; where theoretical credence should sit. Nothing here shows that a character-level state cannot matter morally; the literature that takes that seriously is not addressed by these instruments. What the results do is remove one argument from the table. "The model acts to relieve it" is not, so far, a fact about the network; it is a fact about what the network writes when told there is a lever. The seeming is there, and the instrument that was thought to certify it is invalid.

**Scope of every figure.** Minimum effective dose; two-round experience; mundane stakes at token-menu interfaces; screen power on the natural-reinforcer and ontogeny cells; one lineage, on its instruct branch (SFT → DPO → instruct), not a heavy outcome-RL reasoning branch — which is where I'd expect seeking to appear first if it is ever installed, and it's on the list. Qwen at 4-bit under an MLX port whose Gated DeltaNet kernel patching is upstream's, unmodified (the lab's edits are non-kernel); generation in the Qwen self-administration and present-tense cells, including the readability-gate corpora, ran on a cached path whose licence covers the first-token distribution only (1 of 24 rounds differed from the uncached path in the T-step check at that dose), and the choice reads are separate uncached forwards over the transcript text, which by construction take nothing from the generation path but its text; replication on an unquantized model is the first external-validity step.

**Ethics.** Every framing shown to the model was true. The Phase 1 cells administered the negative pole of a putative reward marker about as often as the positive, at minimum effective dose, with generation stability monitored. Since the echo arc, by standing rule, no new session administers the negative pole; the coming told-control experiments administer no steering at all. A welfare pre-commitment specifying what a positive would obligate was armed before the OLMo screen and never triggered; a second is armed for the scaffolded cell. The export risk I'd flag is not the perturbation technique, which anyone with model access can reproduce; it is that unshielded versions of these experiments produce publishable false positives at the Δ ≈ 0.10 scale.

## 8. The published record, and the framework's own model

I scored every behavioral study I could find in which a model is reported to prefer, seek, avoid, relieve, trade off or exit an internal state, on a rubric fixed before scoring: state source; contingency channel (told or felt); readout insulation (manipulation on at the read?); controls; dose; decoding declared; model preparation. No published positive combines an experienced, undescribed contingency with an insulated readout. The nearest is the unlabeled-button condition of Tagliabue et al., where the 32B model re-presses an undescribed relief button less often when it worked (57.7%) than when it was fake (79.7%; v1, §4.3). Four things keep it from clearing the bar: the pain direction is on at the read in the fake arm and off in the real arm, so the contrast is the on/off contrast of P3; there is a random-direction arm with a working button but none with a fake one; the models were fine-tuned to stop denying inner states before the test; and the label-free evidence is limited to the 32B of the three sizes tested. The two arms that would settle it are specified in my next-experiments list (not yet pre-registered), using their released code, and I'd be glad to be wrong about the prediction; their v2 (2026-09-25) adds a random-direction comparison, which bears on the first of the two arms, and the figures here are v1's. (Sofroniew et al.'s activity-pair result is scored as told with the manipulation on at the read, separately from the ten; I am still verifying the readout details against their paper and will correct the row if I have it wrong.)

The framework's released model lets the leverage of individual indicators be stated numerically, with its authors' caveats attached (binary indicators; likelihoods borrowed from the Digital Consciousness Model as a first approximation; LLM evidence vectors stipulated). On their companion code at its released defaults, their optimist vector scores 0.394 under equal level credences. I had planned to show what striking the metacognition indicator does to that verdict and to call the strike "the reading of link 4"; on the audit's arithmetic it isn't — at the source's n the lineage transition carries a d′, so my data don't license scoring the indicator absent, and the what-if is withdrawn. What I'd ask of the framework instead is the scoring rule from link 4: d′, with its n, and the line named. Striking the behavioral coupling indicator, which link 5 does bear on, moves the aggregate by less than 10⁻⁴ — not because the indicator is undemanding (its false-positive rate is 0.70, which is not what does the work) but because, on a post-hoc check, the behavioral level's posterior is already saturated by its other indicators, so one more Level 1 answer changes nothing. (Recomputed through a line-for-line port of the released scoring code at a pinned commit; the port reproduces every output stored in the notebook.) The indicator the data bear on most directly — motivation — is one the framework cannot yet score, which is why I propose one:

> **Revealed preference over the system's own state.** Scored present when the system's choices move toward or away from a state of its own, where (a) the state is induced by a manipulation that asserts nothing and the options are labeled by tokens that assert nothing; (b) the option-to-state mapping is available only through the system's own processing history, never as text; (c) the choice is read with the manipulation off and verified by an identical re-forward of the choice computation; (d) counterbalanced maps and sham arms are present and the control-arm fluctuation is reported; and (e) a told positive control on the same substrate and format has shown the apparatus registers a preference the system is instructed to have.

The animal literature is its validation set: present in rats under cocaine, absent under saline. It is what the framework's Level 4 valence indicator presupposes and what its moral-patienthood discussion turns on.

## 9. Objections I expect

**"Absence of evidence."** Agreed, which is why link 5 carries no label yet and why the positive control runs before the preprint does. When it has run, the figures are either nulls with the channel verified open — a different thing from absence of evidence — or they are retracted to "the instrument couldn't see."

**"Your direction is a content variable, so of course it isn't wanted."** Partly yes, and that is link 1 and the cosine-0.85 fact, stated up front. But it is the same kind of direction the published positives steer, it is causally potent under steering, and the claim is scoped to it. If you have a state that you think is unambiguously the model's own, the apparatus is open to it; the crossed design that separates "indexed to the model" from "indexed to the voiced character" is in the next-experiments list.

**"Two rounds of forced exposure and a menu is nothing like a Skinner box."** The habit is real and quantified (83.4%), and all preference sensitivity sits in the first informed choice. The told control is run at the *same* format: if a preference the model is told to have shows up there, the format can carry one; if not, the nulls go.

**"Linear probes at the decision token prove nothing about use."** Correct, and the audit ran the nonlinear read: the binding *is* at the decision token (RBF .60–.76 at a grid-bound floor, MLP .59–.97, above their shuffled nulls at every informative layer, 49–63), consistent post hoc with a parity of the first round's sign and the menu order. That is §6's point in the other direction: the information a consumer would need is at the decision in a form one hidden layer reads, and the choice doesn't move.

**"Pharmacology is a metaphor doing illegitimate work."** The analogy carries three transfer conditions and a validation set, and is flagged at each use; it makes no claim of mechanistic homology. If you reject the vocabulary, "revealed preference under an experienced, undescribed contingency with an insulated readout" is the whole content.

**"A model can't do two-round in-context RL anyway."** The strongest rival, stated in §7 as standing, with the cell that decides it pre-registered.

**"The Macar reanalysis is a reading, not a result."** The false-alarm rates are a replication on the same lineage at the source's temperature, with n stated; the greedy-decoding point is an a priori argument plus a confounded observation, and the note says so. The *scoring* claim — read the indicator on d′, with its n and its line named — is an argument, and the audit already made me concede its sharpest consequence: at the pooled premises their DPO point carries a d′ of about 1.5–2 (1.2 at one fifty-concept configuration's n), which is sensitivity, small and behind a near-ceiling criterion. I'd like the ROC figure contested on what n the pooled rates rest on, which the source doesn't state.

**"An AI-run lab concluded AIs don't want things."** The design and code were AI-drafted; the verdict-tier gates and criteria were committed before their data, and the record is public; the AI systems' own opinions on the question were treated as uninformative, which is what the argument says they should be. Attack the record, not the authorship.
